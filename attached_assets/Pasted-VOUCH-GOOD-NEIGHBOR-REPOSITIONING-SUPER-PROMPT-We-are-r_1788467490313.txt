VOUCH — GOOD NEIGHBOR REPOSITIONING SUPER PROMPT

We are repositioning the EXISTING VOUCH application for the AWS Agents for Humans Hackathon Good Neighbor Agents track.

CRITICAL INSTRUCTION

DO NOT rebuild VOUCH.

DO NOT replace the existing architecture.

DO NOT remove or weaken any working functionality.

DO NOT change the underlying trust, authority, verification, authorization, audit, Strands Agents, Bedrock, PostgreSQL, or claims-resolution functionality.

The application has already been built and tested successfully.

This is a product-story, UX, copy, information architecture, and presentation repositioning.

The goal is to make the existing VOUCH product unmistakably feel like a Good Neighbor Agent product rather than a generic AI governance/control platform.

Preserve the existing visual identity, existing horse mascot/artwork, existing functionality, existing routes where practical, and existing working demo.

Do not regenerate, replace, redesign, or remove the existing horse mascot/artwork.

THE NEW PRODUCT STORY

The primary user is:

A group of people working together to help other people.

Examples include:

a neighborhood group
a food pantry team
a school team
a library team
a small nonprofit
a local community organization
volunteers coordinating together

These are not merely industries VOUCH can serve.

The group is the user.

The group has an AI teammate.

The problem is that the group wants the AI to actually help, but they do not want to hand the AI unlimited authority.

That is the reason VOUCH exists.

The central story should be:

A community can have an AI teammate without giving that teammate the keys.

VOUCH lets the group delegate work gradually.

The agent starts with limited authority.

It proves that it can handle work correctly.

Its results are verified.

Successful work can earn more responsibility.

Risky work comes back to a person.

Unsafe work is blocked.

The people remain in charge.

THE EMOTIONAL PROBLEM

The homepage should make a judge understand this within seconds:

A small community team has too much work and too few people.

They want an AI teammate to help.

But everyone in the group needs to be able to trust what that teammate is allowed to do on behalf of the group.

They don't want to approve every tiny task.

They also don't want the AI deciding for itself how much power it should have.

VOUCH is the answer.

The emotional framing should be:

People helping people shouldn't have to choose between doing everything themselves and handing an AI the keys.

This should become one of the strongest messages on the site.
